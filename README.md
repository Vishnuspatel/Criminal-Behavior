This project focused on predicting criminal activity using a variety of factors related to alcohol and drug use, education, employment, and more. The data was sourced from the 2021 National Survey on Drug Use and Health (NSDUH). We explored different combinations of variables by including or excluding certain predictors and applying transformations to improve model performance. The final project included seven models with accuracy ranging from 60% to 70%. Additional performance metrics were also evaluated, including sensitivity, specificity, false positive rate (FPR), false negative rate (FNR), root mean squared error (RMSE), and mean absolute error (MAE). After reviewing these results, we found that our top models performed similarly in terms of out-of-sample error and accuracy. We additionally evaluated Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC), which produced similar results. Ultimately, we selected the simpler model over the more complex alternative to reduce the potential for overfitting. Overall, the project was a success and the final model achieved an accuracy of approximately 86%. This project could be expanded in the future by implementing cross-validation methods, expanding the subset of variables analyzed, and introducing new data collection methods to include populations not represented in the original survey.
